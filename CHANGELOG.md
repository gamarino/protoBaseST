# Changelog

## 0.4.0 (unreleased)

### Requirements

- **protoST 0.16.1** (was 0.12.0), **protoCore 3.6.1** (was 3.6.0) and **protoIO 0.7.1** (was
  0.7.0). protoST 0.14.0 adds the exact `Fraction` comparison and
  `ByteArray >> unsignedIntegerFrom:to:bigEndian:`; 0.14.1 fixes S43 (a ThreadSanitizer
  capped-wait polling flake) and S44 (empty conversions); 0.15.0 adds the `Float fsum:`,
  `fsumOfProducts:with:` and `fsumOfSquaredDifferences:with:` primitives, which the vector scores
  call, and 0.16.0 `Float fsumOfPowSquaredDifferences:with:`, the IVF k-means distance
  (`pow(a_i - b_i, 2.0)`, then the msum of `fsum:`); 0.16.1 makes a helping wait run one message at
  a time (S48) and, with protoIO 0.7.1, bounds a whole socket write by its timeout and reports the
  bytes written (S49), which the TCP transport's fix needs ("Fixed" below). The README
  Requirements table, the tutorial and the examples state the floor.
- **The floor is checked when the package loads** (final review I1; `src/protobase/floor.st`).
  `Import from: 'protobase'` probes, right after the errors and before any other module, for what
  protoBaseST calls of the floor: `ConnectionTimedOut >> bytesWritten` (protoST 0.16.1, protoIO
  0.7.1) and the four `Float` primitives of the vector kernel (`fsum:`, `fsumOfProducts:with:`,
  `fsumOfSquaredDifferences:with:`, 0.15.0; `fsumOfPowSquaredDifferences:with:`, 0.16.0). On a
  protoST that lacks one it signals `PBNotSupportedError`: "protoBaseST 0.4.0 requires protoST
  0.16.1 or later (protoIO 0.7.1); this protoST lacks ...", naming each missing capability.
  Before, the floor was stated but not checked: on protoST 0.16.0 the TCP sender's write timeout
  asked `ConnectionTimedOut` for `bytesWritten`, a `MessageNotUnderstood` its drain caught as a
  stray error, so a frame under back-pressure was dropped with no word naming the cause (the
  review measured `cluster_transport_tcp_slow_reader.st` on 0.16.0: exit 124); below 0.15.0 the
  first vector score signalled `MessageNotUnderstood`. Checked by hand on 2026-11-23: protoST
  0.16.0 and 0.8.1 refuse with that text; tests `floor_check.st`,
  `floor_refuses_without_bytes_written.st` and `floor_refuses_without_kmeans_primitive.st` (the
  last two simulate the missing capability in the test file only).
- **The floor moved during the cycle.** The vectors phase ran on a 0.14.1 floor, with each
  primitive behind a feature probe and the Smalltalk kernel as the fallback; the TCP fix then
  moved the floor to 0.16.1 (a split floor, 0.14.1 standalone and 0.16.1 for TCP clusters, would
  be a trap). On 0.16.1 every primitive is always present, so the probes (`hasFsumPrimitive`,
  `hasPowSquaresPrimitive`) are gone and `PBVectorMath usesPrimitives` replaces
  `usesFsumPrimitive` and `usesPowSquaresPrimitive`. The Smalltalk kernel stays, as the tests'
  oracle (`_forceSmalltalkKernel:` runs every vendored case on both paths, the centroids
  byte-identical) and where a primitive cannot answer (an Integer term, sequences of unequal
  sizes). The vector benchmarks ran on 0.16.0, whose vector primitives 0.16.1 leaves unchanged.
- **The final suite** ran on protoST 0.16.1 (the floor), protoCore 3.6.1 and protoIO 0.7.1, with
  both interop venvs present, after the TCP fix; the earlier final runs of task V9 (protoST 0.14.1,
  0.14.2 and 0.16.0, and the vector contract files at 32 property seeds) are kept below. The
  results are under "Final verification".

### Vectors

- **The vector math kernel** (`src/protobase/vectors.st`, task V1; internal, not public API):
  `PBVectorMath` computes what `proto_db` v2.0.0rc4 computes with CPython, bit for bit:
  Python's `float()` of Integers and `Decimal`s (one rounding, subnormals included; nil beyond the
  Float range; a signalling NaN refused with CPython's text), `canonical_vector`, CPython's
  `math.fsum` (a line-for-line port of `math_fsum`, its special values and two errors), the three
  scores, `score_floor`, `rank_entries` (ties by the member key's order key), `_normalized`,
  `_training_order`, and `_kmeans` on its pure-Python path, whose square is libm `pow`
  (`raisedTo: 2`), never `x * x`. `PBPythonRandom` ports CPython's MT19937 seeding, `getrandbits`,
  `_randbelow` and both branches of `sample`. Pinned by `tests/unit/vec_math.st`,
  `tests/unit/vec_random.st` and 4021 vendored `proto_db` vectors
  (`tests/conformance/vector_math.st`, generated by `tests/interop/py_vector_math_cases.py` with
  numpy blocked; `tests/conformance/fetch_vector_math_vectors.sh`).
- **Near queries and `near[]` terms** (task V2): `PBNearQuery` (`src/protobase/vectors.st`), the
  value of `proto_db`'s `near[]` term, validated with `proto_db`'s texts (CPython's where `proto_db`
  fails with a Python error: an Integer beyond the Float range, a signalling-NaN `Decimal`, an
  unhashable metric; DV7), equal and hashed with Python's float equality. `PBTerm path:op:value:`
  accepts `'near[]'` (a `PBNearQuery`, or a mapping of its fields), so the term is no longer refused
  when built; `PBExpression compile:` and `asList` read and write both list forms, `describe` renders
  the query as `proto_db` does; `matches:` (so `evaluate:`) is `PBNotSupportedError`, a `PBFilter`
  holding a `near[]` term is `PBValidationError`, and `PBPlanner normalize:` refuses a negated one.
  Plans answer `near[]` since V6 and V7. Pinned by `tests/unit/vec_terms.st` and 42 `near[]` cases
  added to the term vector (`tests/interop/py_term_cases.py`,
  `tests/conformance/vectors-index-keys/terms.msgpack`).
- **Vector indexes, exact and hnsw** (task V3): `PBVectorIndex` (`src/protobase/indexes.st`) is a
  port of `proto_db`'s `VectorIndex`: `name:keySpec:dim:metric:backend:params:` and its conveniences
  validate the definition in `proto_db`'s order with its texts (parameters completed and stored as
  sorted pairs; `retrain_growth` an Integer beyond the Float range refused with CPython's text,
  DV7 e); `withAdded:`, `withRemoved:`, `withReplaced:with:`, `empty` and `build:` maintain the store
  and the invalid group node for node as `proto_db` does, node classes included (an invalid tree
  built in bulk stays a `KeyNode` tree, one grown from empty is a `CountedKeyNode` tree); `validate:`
  and `explain` are `proto_db`'s (a stored total or vector component compared with Python's `==`:
  `true` is 1); a signalling-NaN `Decimal` component of a stored element is refused with CPython's
  text (DV7 b). The backends are `PBVectorExact`, `PBVectorIvfFlat` (its parameter checks and
  explain entries; lists and training are V4's, refused naming the index until then) and
  `PBVectorHnsw` (`src/protobase/vectors.st`). Every value a vector refusal quotes, `PBNearQuery`'s
  included, is rendered as Python's `repr` (`True`, `None`, `(1.0,)`, `[1, 2]`) by one helper,
  `PBCodec _pyRepr:`, moved from `PBTerm` (where `describe` uses it) to `src/protobase/codec.st`.
  `PBMemberKeys checkDepth:memberKey:index:outer:` counts the store key's two containers. Its
  collections were still read only until V5 (below), and no index answers `near[]` yet (V6). Pinned by `tests/unit/vec_index.st`, `tests/interop/vector_index_identical.sh` (48 indexes
  of dims 3, 1 and 16, `build:`, seeded hook sequences and the three invalid-tree histories, byte
  for byte, and 59 refusal texts), and the `vectors`, `hnsw` and `ivf` roots of
  `tests/conformance/database_contents.st`.
- **Benchmark VB0** (`bench/run_query.sh VB0`, `bench/vector_workload.st`,
  `bench/py_vector_workload.py`, `bench/py_vector_data.py`): the kernel alone, fsum against a naive

- **IVF-Flat lists, training and retraining** (task V4): `PBVectorIvfFlat` `listFor:vector:`

  int-against-float comparison), `train:vectors:` (vectors sorted by their bytes, the pure-Python

- **The fsum primitives of protoST 0.15.0** (task VP-adopt; internal): `PBVectorMath` computes

  pure-Python one (`docs/deviations.md`, Performance, "Vectors").

  `tests/unit/vec_math.st` (case 17) and by every score case of `tests/conformance/vector_math.st`

- **Benchmarks VB3 and VB5** (`bench/run_query.sh VB3 VB5`, `bench/_vector_bench.st`): appends
  committed to a vector-indexed list of 600 and 5000, and blind writes merged onto a
  vector-indexed dictionary, standalone and in a three-member cluster, self-verified against
  `proto_db`'s checksums (the IVF centroids included). On protoST 0.16.0 an IVF retrain dominates:
  5.0 commits per second against 16.2 with an exact index and 35.9 with none (the run of task V5's
  fix round 2, 2026-11-21; 3.3, 16.6 and 36.7 on 0.15.0; the full comparison of 2026-11-22, which
  chapter 20 cites, measured 4.6, 15.2 and 34.5); a retrain over 630 to 700 members takes 1.6 to 1.9 s, so in a cluster, where a retrain
  longer than the write permission (667 ms with the default lease) never commits, an IVF index
  needs a lease of more than four times the measured retrain for its size (2.5 ms per member at
  631 rising to 3.7 at 5150: count up to about 4 ms, so 11 s for 700 members, 80 s for 5000). That
  rule is the break-even point, a lower bound with no margin (final review M6): the permission is a
  third of the lease less sendReserve, a twelfth by default, so a retrain that takes a quarter of
  the lease leaves nothing over, and retrains slow by about half as a process ages (deviations,
  "Vectors"); size the lease at twice the rule, eight times the measured retrain (about 32 ms per
  member: 22 s for 700 members, 160 s for 5000). Only liveness is at stake: a commit whose retrain
  outlasts the permission is refused, retryably, naming `leaseDuration`, and writes nothing
  (`tests/unit/cluster_vector_retrain.st`, final review M7). VB5's IVF
  cluster unit runs with a 90 s lease, which lengthens the write outage when a member dies

  `proto_db`'s default mode (numpy, `tests/interop/.venv-numpy`) is a column of the VB3 and VB5

- **The planner's `near[]` rules and `PBField near:`** (task V7): `PBPlanner` has `proto_db`'s
  `near[]` rules (`planner.py:98-135`, `194-275`): a `near[]` conjunct is always answered by an
  index (`PBVectorSearch` over the cheapest that supports it), never by a filter, and is
  `PBNotSupportedError` with `proto_db`'s text when none supports it; one bare `near(k)` with other

  conjuncts are intersected; a disjunction holding one is always a `PBUnion`, its branches without

### Deviations added

Each is a row of `docs/deviations.md` ("Indexed queries" and Performance, "Vectors"), with its
reason and cost; the addendum's DV1 to DV9:

- **DV1, hnsw search refused** (ruling VQ1): maintained, merged, validated, explained, estimated
  and planned; `PBNotSupportedError` at the first execution of its plan, as `proto_db` without
  hnswlib.
- **DV2, the exact backend's `numpy`** is kept and written, with no effect.
- **DV3, IVF training** is `proto_db`'s pure-Python k-means (the libm note, the two ways the square
  is reached, MSVC's `pow`, protoST D58); centroids may differ in the last bits from `proto_db`'s
  numpy training; either side validates the other's index.

### Fixed

- **A TCP cluster node could hang a program whose actors wait while a connection is open**
  (0.2.0 to 0.3.0; fixed in 0.4.0, which needs protoST 0.16.1 and protoIO 0.7.1). On a node whose
  transport is `PBTcpTransport`, an actor that waits inside a block (a commit does:
  `PBTransaction commit` waits inside `ensure:`) lets its worker run other actors' messages on top
  of the wait (protoST LANGUAGE 10.4). The listener's accept loop and each connection's read loop
  were one message that lasted as long as the transport or the connection, so a waiting actor that
  picked one up stayed buried, after what it waited for had settled, until the transport stopped,
  and a program that waited for it with no deadline before stopping the transport hung. Reproduced
  with five transports and eight such actors at one worker, two workers and the default pool on
  protoST 0.14.1, and at one worker and the default pool on 0.16.0. Every turn of the transport's actors now ends: the listener's after
  one accept that waits at most `pollInterval` (50 ms), a reader's after reads of at most that
  long (a frame read in part is kept for the next turn), a sender's after writes of at most that
  long (the rest of a frame continues in the next turn), or after one connect, which waits up to
  `requestTimeout` (1 s by default) for a peer that does not answer (final review M3: with a
  black-holed peer, whose dropped SYN is never refused, the connect is retried after every
  back-off of 0.1 to 0.2 s, so a waiting actor can stay buried for up to `requestTimeout` again and
  again; no test exercises that wait, the tests' unreachable peer being a closed loopback port,
  which refuses at once; `docs/not-done.md`); each actor then sends itself the next
  turn, and `stop` chains on a Future each actor settles when its last turn ends. The bytes, their
  order, the counters, the errors, the reconnects and the timeouts are unchanged; a frame's write
  still fails after `requestTimeout` of writing, now counted over its writes. The fix needs protoST
  0.16.1, whose helping wait runs one message and then checks its Future (on 0.16.0 the actor's
  self-sent turns kept the wait buried, and at one worker stopped the program), and protoIO 0.7.1,
  whose write timeout bounds a whole write and reports the bytes written (before, a large write to
  a peer that did not read could block without limit). These turns starve no other actor: each
  blocks in I/O with a timeout, and the pool adds a worker while one does (protoST D59). Tests:
  `cluster_transport_tcp_burial.st` (the reproducer: it hangs on 0.16.0 and passes on 0.16.1, at
  one worker and the default pool), `cluster_transport_tcp_starvation.st` (independent actors
  progress at one worker while connections poll), `cluster_transport_tcp_partial_frame.st` and
  `cluster_transport_tcp_slow_reader.st` (a timed-out write keeps the unwritten rest, or the
  attempt fails exactly as before), each with a one-worker script.
  **What the finite reads cost** (final review M4; `bench/run_tcp_frames.sh`, 2026-11-23, protoST
  0.16.1, three rounds, five frames per size, a frame offered already encoded so only the transport
  is timed): a reader reads a body 1 KiB at a time (`readChunk`, which bounds how long a peer that
  trickles bytes keeps a reader's turn, see `docs/not-done.md`), against one read per body in
  0.3.0. The minimum delivery time of each round: 3 to 4 ms for a 64 KiB frame (1 to 2 with one
  read per body), 7 to 8 ms for 256 KiB (4), 27 to 32 ms for 1 MiB (11 to 13) and 58 to 67 ms for
  2 MiB (20 to 24): the 1 KiB reads cost about 2 to 3 times the one-read delivery from 256 KiB up.
  A cluster's frames are small except for large records in replication, and the encoding of a
  large message costs more than its transport (the review's end-to-end figures, encoding included:
  1.1 to 1.4 times 0.3.0's at 1 and 2 MiB). Indicative, one machine.
  **Upgrade note for programs on protoBaseST 0.2.0 to 0.3.0** (or on a protoST older than 0.16.1):
  on a node with a TCP transport, do not wait with no deadline for an actor that waits inside a
  block (an actor that commits) while the transport runs: stop the transport first (`stop`, and
  wait on its Future), then wait for the actor, which then returns; or wait with `waitWithin:`,
  which signals `TimeoutError` instead of hanging. Committing from the program thread is not
  exposed.

- **`stop` could wait for ever after a close that signalled** (final review M1; new in this
  cycle's transport). A reader's last turn, a sender's drain and `stop` settled their `ended`
  Future after closing the socket, in the same block, so a close that signalled (protoST signals
  on closing a Socket without a descriptor) left `ended`, and so `stop`'s Future, unsettled. Each
  `ended` now settles in an `ensure:` around the close, and a close that fails is written to
  stderr and goes no further (`PBClusterTransport _close:`); `MessageNotUnderstood` still passes.
  Test: `cluster_transport_tcp_failures.st` (1) to (4).
- **A socket of the TCP transport could be closed twice** (final review M9; 0.2.0 onward). `stop`
  closed every accepted socket from the caller's thread while the connection's reader could close
  it too, and a sender's connection likewise; `IOStream >> close` is not atomic (its `closed` flag
  is a plain instance variable), so both could release the descriptor, the second perhaps after the
  number was given to another descriptor. Each socket is now closed exactly once, by whichever
  closer wins a compare-and-set (`_PBTcpReader >> closeSocket`, `_PBTcpSender >> _closeLink`).
  The non-atomic close is protoST's (and protoIO's close of a descriptor never read or written
  through a state); it is recorded for them in `docs/not-done.md`. Test:
  `cluster_transport_tcp_failures.st` (5) and (6).
- **The listener spun when an accept failed at once** (final review M10; as in 0.3.0 and
  `proto_db`'s `_accept` loop). An accept that fails at once (`EMFILE`, for example) was written to
  stderr and the next turn sent at once, one line per turn without a pause. The next turn now
  follows a timer of `pollInterval`; the turn itself still ends at once. Test:
  `cluster_transport_tcp_failures.st` (7) and (8).

## 0.2.0 (2026-11-19)

The detailed, task-by-task log of this release is
[docs/changelog-0.2.0-development.md](docs/changelog-0.2.0-development.md).

### Cluster mode (new)

protoBaseST members can now share one database as a cluster, following `proto_db` 2.0.0rc4's
cluster protocol 14 at storage format 4. A commit is acknowledged once a majority of the voters
accepted it, so a cluster of three survives the loss of any one member. See tutorial chapters
13-16 and `examples/cluster/`.

- **Starting a member:** `PBClusterCoordinator nodeId:peers:options:` (options in milliseconds,
  validated at construction: `PBClusterConfig`), then `PBObjectSpace storage: (PBFileStorage
  clusterPath: dir) coordinator: aCoordinator`. Members talk over TCP (`PBTcpTransport`), or over
  `PBInProcessNetwork` in tests. The optional `#authKey` authenticates every frame with HMAC; the
  transport is not encrypted.
- **Committing and reading** work as in standalone mode: `run:`, `commit`, `listDatabases` and
  `openDatabase:`. A commit may end in `PBOutcomeUnknownError` when its root left the node and its
  fate could not be learnt; `run:` never retries it. A member left in a minority gets
  `PBTimeoutError`.
- **Durability and state:** `committedEpoch`, `persistedEpoch`, `sync:` (a durability barrier) and
  `persisterStats` on the space. On the coordinator: `stats`, `isHealthy`, `owner`,
  `clusterSize`, `voters`, `isVoter`, `majority`, and `forceRejoin` for the operator's decision
  when a majority of acceptor logs is lost.
- **Failures:** a member restarted on its disk keeps its promises and waits out its startup grace.
  A member whose acceptor log was lost rejoins by a ballot. An internal error fences the member
  until its process restarts. A failed start leaves the coordinator stopped and signals the
  cause.
- **An existing standalone database can be brought into a cluster** by opening its directory with
  `clusterPath:`. The move is one way: `PBFileStorage path:` refuses the directory afterwards
  (chapter 16).
- **Interoperability:** `proto_db` reads a space a protoBaseST cluster wrote, and the acceptor
  logs round-trip both ways. Mixed clusters of protoBaseST and `proto_db` members are not a goal.
- **Not included:** tiered storage (its options are refused), pipelined permissions (one
  permission at a time across the cluster), dynamic membership, and storage format 5, which is
  refused with `PBNotSupportedError`. Every difference from `proto_db` is in
  `docs/deviations.md`, and `docs/not-done.md` states the cluster's cuts and what is unproven
  (the final verification: the long soak ran every simulator family at commit 02d9826 on protoCore
  3.6.0 and protoST 0.12.0: sim_harness, sim_base_choice, sim_rejoin, sim_base_seeds_a,
  sim_base_seeds_b and sim_grants at 5000 seeds each, sim_leases_terms_a and sim_leases_terms_b at
  2000, sim_acceptance and sim_permits at 1000, sim_round_rules at 400 and sim_leases at 100 (five
  families one after another, then the other seven at once; each run is deterministic per seed), and
  every one passed; the full timer audit (81 parts, 3420 simulator runs) ran twice at the final
  code: the first run had no violation but missed 36 floors by 1 to 7 commits, because 02d9826
  changes every schedule (bisected), so the floors were re-measured from it (7cbac4a); the second
  run passed whole at 7cbac4a in 2 h 22 min, serially).
- **Costs:** on one machine, a cluster commits at about half the standalone rate for small commits
  on disjoint roots, and a third on a hot key (`bench/run_cluster.sh`; indicative figures in
  `docs/deviations.md`, Performance).

### Standalone mode

- **Group commit:** the commits queued on one space are published together, with one root lock,
  one flush and one publish per round (`proto_db` D52). A single commit runs the same protocol as
  before.
- **Format:** follows `proto_db` 2.0.0rc4 at format 4. Format 5 segments, roots and logs are
  refused with `PBNotSupportedError`.

### Documentation

- A tutorial suite: twelve small programs in `examples/tutorial/`, each with a chapter in
  `docs/tutorial/`, plus "Coming from GemStone/S" and "Coming from Soil". The ERP walk-through
  moved to `docs/tutorial/erp.md`.
- Cluster chapters 13-16 and their programs in `examples/cluster/`.
- `docs/reference.md` covers the cluster API and its timer table. The documentation tests check
  that the chapters quote their programs exactly and that every selector they cite exists.
